# The NET track, summarised across every configuration tried (2026-09-08)

Eight runs today, each resuming from the same champion (`champion_long.net`, 0.864 +/- 0.013 vs a
frozen origin) and each finally scored against that same frozen origin at 600 pairs.

| run | varied | accepts | mean gate rate | origin score | vs 0.864 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gp_40 | gate 40 pairs | 7 | 0.5116 | 0.826 | **-0.038 WORSE** |
| gp_224 | gate 224 pairs | 1 | 0.5021 | 0.859 | -0.005 dead heat |
| rp_1 | replay window 1 | 6 | 0.5128 | 0.843 | **-0.021 WORSE** |
| rp_8 | replay window 8 | 11 | 0.5275 | 0.862 | -0.002 dead heat |
| rp_999 | replay unlimited | 8 | 0.5212 | 0.863 | -0.001 dead heat |
| ep_1 | 1 epoch | 4 | 0.5036 | 0.834 | **-0.030 WORSE** |
| ep_2 | 2 epochs | 3 | 0.5087 | 0.863 | -0.001 dead heat |
| ep_3 | 3 epochs | 1 | 0.5011 | 0.859 | -0.005 dead heat |

**Not one run is above its starting point.** Three are resolved worse, five are dead heats, none
better. That spans gate sizes 32/40/224, replay windows 1/8/999 and training budgets 1/2/3/10/30 --
every axis touched today.

## The structural observation

**Mean gate rate is >= 0.5 in ALL EIGHT runs** (0.5011 to 0.5275). The gate plays each candidate
against its own champion over 224 pairs, so on average candidates DO beat the net they came from --
in every configuration. And the accumulated lineage still never improves against a fixed opponent.

**Beating the champion is not the same as getting stronger.** That is not a subtlety here, it is
the whole result: the loop's objective and its measured outcome point in different directions, and
`corr(accepts, origin score) = -0.075` says the number of promotions carries essentially no
information about where the lineage lands.

## Hypothesis, explicitly NOT established

A candidate is trained on data GENERATED BY its champion. Learning to predict that specific
opponent's play would produce exactly this signature: wins against the parent, no absolute gain.
That is opponent-overfitting, and it is a HYPOTHESIS -- nothing here measures it. Testing it means
scoring candidates against an opponent they were not trained against, which the origin control
already does at the END of a run but never per-generation.

It does NOT explain everything either: epochs 10 and 30 have gate rates BELOW 0.5 (0.4715, 0.4537),
so more training makes candidates worse at beating the champion AND worse absolutely. Any account
of this has to cover both halves.

## What is now closed

* Gate resolution: fixed and validated (40 -> 224; the blunt gate scored 0.826 vs 0.859).
* The accept/reject surrogate: measured at corr -0.095 against 239 paired gate results, no strength
  signal, now off by default.
* Replay window: not resolved at any width; 8 and 999 are indistinguishable.
* Training budget: more is monotonically worse, measured in games.
* Under-fitting, draws-in-surrogate, horizon-widening: all proposed by me and all REFUTED.

## What is open

Whether "candidate beats champion" can be made to mean "candidate is stronger". Every configuration
knob has now been turned without moving the origin score, so the next question is about the
OBJECTIVE, not its parameters.

# NON-TRANSITIVITY: DEMONSTRATED, not inferred (2026-09-08)

The hypothesis above -- "beating the champion is not the same as getting stronger" -- was inferred
from eight runs. It is now measured directly, with `control --opponent`:

| match | result | resolved? |
|---|---|---|
| `ep_1` vs `champion_long` (head to head, 600 pairs, depth 2) | **0.545 +/- 0.018** | YES, excludes 0.5 |
| `champion_long` vs frozen origin | 0.864 +/- 0.013 | — |
| `ep_1` vs frozen origin | 0.834 +/- 0.013 | YES, 0.030 gap vs a 0.018 floor |

**ep_1 BEATS champion_long, and champion_long BEATS ep_1 against a common third opponent.** Both
directions clear their intervals. This is an intransitive cycle, measured on the real nets the loop
produced, at the exact depth the loop's own gate uses.

## Why this is the root cause and not another symptom

The loop's accept rule is "does the candidate beat the current champion". This result says that
quantity can move in the OPPOSITE direction to strength against a fixed opponent. So the loop can
run perfectly -- gate correctly sized, surrogate disabled, every candidate honestly measured -- and
still walk sideways or downhill, because it is climbing the wrong quantity.

It explains the whole table above without any additional assumption:

* mean gate rate >= 0.5 in **all eight** runs: candidates really do beat their parents.
* not one final net above the 0.864 start, three resolved worse: those wins do not accumulate into
  strength.
* `corr(accepts, origin score) = -0.075`: promoting more of them changes nothing, because what is
  being promoted is not what is being measured at the end.

## What it does NOT say

* NOT that the gate is broken. The gate measures candidate-vs-champion accurately; that is the
  problem, not a defect in it.
* NOT that self-play cannot work. Intransitivity is a known property of self-play objectives, and
  the standard responses are to score against a FIXED reference or a POOL of past opponents rather
  than only the current champion.
* NOT that ep_1 is a bad net in some absolute sense -- it is better than champion_long at playing
  champion_long, which is exactly what it was trained to be.

## Next, and it is a design change rather than a parameter

Gate candidates against something that does not move: the frozen origin, or a pool of past
champions, or both alongside the champion match. The origin control already exists and is already
used to score runs at the END -- the change is to consult it during the accept decision, where the
whole day's evidence says the champion match alone is insufficient.
